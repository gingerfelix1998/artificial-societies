# Freedman 2013 — Strategy: A History

## Argument

**This summary is thin and the work has little bearing on a 1962 crisis scenario.**

A very long general history of strategic thought from antiquity through military, political and
business strategy to the present. Its recurring argument is that strategy is less the execution of
a plan than the creation of power through the sequencing of moves, and that grand designs
consistently fail because adversaries adapt — so strategy is better understood as a narrative
maintained under revision than as an architecture.

The nuclear material is a chapter within a much larger sweep and adds nothing to what *The
Evolution of Nuclear Strategy* covers in depth.

Recommendation: exclude from the panel corpus. If any Freedman persona is built, the 1981 book and
the 2004 conceptual treatment are the sources; this one dilutes rather than adds.

## Claims as stated

- Strategy creates power through the sequencing of moves rather than executing a fixed plan.
- Grand designs fail consistently because adversaries adapt.
- Strategy is better understood as a narrative maintained under revision.

## Verify

Everything above. The work is a synthesis rather than an argument, so claims extracted from it are
unusually likely to be the summariser's rather than the author's.

## Source

Freedman, *Strategy: A History* (Oxford University Press, 2013). Manifest only.
