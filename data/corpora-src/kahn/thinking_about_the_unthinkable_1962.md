# Kahn 1962 — Thinking About the Unthinkable

## Argument

**Availability is uncertain and must be resolved before this document is used.** The book was
published in 1962; whether it appeared before or after October is not established here, and the
whole pre-/post-cutoff logic depends on it. Treat as excluded until the publication month is
confirmed.

Substantively this is Kahn's defence of his own method rather than a new analytical contribution.
Written in response to the reception of *On Thermonuclear War*, it argues that the refusal to
think about nuclear war is itself a policy with consequences, and that the consequences fall on
the side of greater danger rather than less.

The argument has three parts. Refusing to analyse the failure of deterrence does not make failure
less likely; it makes the plans that would be executed on failure worse, because they will have
been made hastily and by people who could not admit what they were doing. Second, moral revulsion
directed at analysis rather than at weapons misdirects the objection: the analyst describes a
situation others created, and suppressing the description leaves the situation intact and less
understood. Third, the alternative to explicit analysis is not the absence of assumptions but
implicit and unexamined ones, and implicit assumptions are worse because they cannot be argued
with.

He also addresses the specific charge that thinking about survival makes war more likely by making
it seem tolerable. His answer is that credibility requires an adversary to believe one would
retaliate, which requires believing one could survive to do so, so the appearance of tolerability
is a component of deterrence rather than a threat to it.

Because the analytical content largely restates *On Thermonuclear War*, its value as an
independent corpus document is limited. Its distinct contribution is the meta-argument about the
propriety of analysis, which is a position on how deliberation should be conducted rather than on
strategy — potentially relevant to a panel where moral objection to analysis was live.
