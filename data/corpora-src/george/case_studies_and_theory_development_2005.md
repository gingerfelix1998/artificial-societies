# George and Bennett 2005 — Case Studies and Theory Development

## Argument

**A methods text with no substantive nuclear content. Not a panel document.**

Written with Andrew Bennett and published after George's death, this is the standard treatment of
qualitative case-study method in political science. Its contributions include the systematic
statement of process tracing — establishing a causal chain within a case rather than inferring
from covariation across cases — and of structured focused comparison, in which the same
theoretically derived questions are asked of every case so that findings are comparable.

It also sets out typological theorising and the use of congruence and counterfactual methods, and
argues that case study and statistical work answer different questions rather than competing.

The relevance to this project is methodological and, in one respect, direct: structured focused
comparison is the same principle as applying a fixed codebook uniformly across subjects, and
process tracing is the standard against which a claim about *why* a decision was taken should be
judged. Both bear on how the simulation's findings could be validated.

Recommendation: exclude from the panel corpus. Retain in the methods bibliography.

## Claims as stated

- Process tracing establishes causal chains within a case rather than inferring from covariation.
- Structured focused comparison asks the same theoretically derived questions of every case.
- Uniform question sets make findings comparable across cases.
- Typological theorising organises cases by configurations of variables.
- Case study and statistical methods answer different questions rather than competing.
