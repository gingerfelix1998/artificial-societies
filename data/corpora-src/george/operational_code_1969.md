# George 1969 — The Operational Code

## Argument

A methodological article proposing a structured way to characterise a political leader's belief
system, and directly relevant to this project as a method rather than as a substantive position.

George's proposal is that a leader's beliefs can be reconstructed as an operational code: a set of
answers to a small number of recurring questions about the political world. The philosophical
beliefs concern the nature of political life — whether conflict is fundamental or transient, how
much of the future is predictable, how much control an actor has over historical development, the
role of chance. The instrumental beliefs concern how to act — the best approach to selecting goals,
how to calculate and control risk, the utility of different means, the timing of action.

The method's appeal is that it produces a comparable structure across leaders rather than a
narrative, so beliefs become variables. Its limitation, which George acknowledges, is that
reconstruction depends on inference from public and private statements, and statements are shaped
by audience.

For this project the article is more useful as a design template than as panel content. It is a
codebook: a fixed small set of dimensions with defined questions, applied uniformly to every
subject, producing a comparable belief structure from textual evidence. That is precisely the
instrument the dimension-coding design requires, and it has an established methodological
literature behind it, including subsequent quantitative operationalisations.

Recommendation: read for method. Whether it belongs in the panel corpus as a persona document is
doubtful — it says little about nuclear strategy.

## Claims as stated

- A leader's beliefs can be reconstructed as a structured operational code.
- Philosophical beliefs concern the nature of political life, predictability, control and chance.
- Instrumental beliefs concern goal selection, risk calculation and control, means and timing.
- A fixed question set produces comparable belief structures across leaders.
- Beliefs so structured become variables rather than narrative.
- Reconstruction depends on inference from statements, which are shaped by audience.

## Verify

Confirm the question set as George states it — later operationalisations by Walker and others
alter it, and the versions are often conflated. Note that George builds on Leites's work on the
Bolshevik operational code, which should be attributed. Little nuclear content; consider excluding
from the panel corpus while retaining as a methodological source.
