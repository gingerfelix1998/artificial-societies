# Posen 1984 — The Sources of Military Doctrine

## Argument

Posen asks why states adopt the military doctrines they do, and tests two explanations against
the interwar cases of France, Britain and Germany. The answer supplies the theoretical basis for
his later escalation work.

Organisation theory predicts that militaries left to themselves will choose offensive doctrines
regardless of strategic circumstance, because offensives preserve organisational autonomy, provide
measurable objectives, support internal hierarchy and justify resources. Balance-of-power theory
predicts doctrine will respond to the external threat environment as statesmen assess it. Neither
alone fits the cases.

His synthesis is that organisational preference is the default and civilian intervention is the
corrective. Militaries drift toward offence and toward doctrinal stability; doctrine changes
significantly when civilians intervene, and civilians intervene when the external situation
becomes threatening enough to overcome their reluctance to interfere in professional military
matters. The interwar variation is explained by differences in the degree and timing of that
intervention, often mediated by a maverick officer allied with civilian authority.

The consequences for nuclear questions are indirect but real. If offensive doctrinal preference is
organisationally generated rather than strategically derived, then a military's operational plans
will systematically overshoot what political leaders would choose, and civilians will discover this
only if they look. That is the premise the 1991 book builds on, and it is also a claim about the
reliability of military advice in a crisis.

## Claims as stated

- Militaries left to themselves choose offensive doctrines regardless of strategic circumstance.
- Offensive doctrine serves organisational autonomy, measurable objectives, hierarchy and resources.
- Militaries also prefer doctrinal stability over adaptation.
- Doctrine changes significantly when civilians intervene.
- Civilians intervene when the external threat overcomes their reluctance to interfere.
- Neither organisation theory nor balance-of-power theory alone explains doctrinal outcomes.
- Doctrinal innovation is often mediated by a maverick officer allied with civilian authority.
- Military operational plans will systematically overshoot what political leaders would choose.

## Verify

Confirm the two theories as Posen labels them and the precise form of his synthesis. Check the
maverick-officer mechanism, which is associated with Posen but also with Stephen Rosen's later
work and may be conflated. The interwar cases carry no nuclear content, so nuclear implications
above are inference and must be marked as such if used.
