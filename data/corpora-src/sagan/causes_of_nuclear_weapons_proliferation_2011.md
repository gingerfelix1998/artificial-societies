# Sagan 2011 — The Causes of Nuclear Weapons Proliferation

## Argument

**This summary is thin and the work has little bearing on a 1962 crisis scenario.**

A review article surveying the state of proliferation scholarship, revisiting and extending the
three-model framework of the 1996 article in light of subsequent quantitative and case-study
work. It assesses what the accumulated evidence supports, notes that the security model retains
substantial but not exclusive support, and argues that supply-side factors and the availability of
sensitive nuclear assistance had been underweighted relative to demand-side explanations.

Its function is historiographical rather than theoretical, and it is a useful map of the
proliferation literature. It contributes nothing to crisis decision-making, and it postdates the
scenario by nearly fifty years.

Recommendation: exclude from the panel corpus. If retained, treat as bibliography rather than as a
source of positions.

## Claims as stated

- The security model retains substantial but not exclusive empirical support.
- Supply-side factors and sensitive nuclear assistance were underweighted in earlier work.
- Demand-side and supply-side explanations are complementary rather than competing.
- Accumulated case and quantitative evidence supports a multi-causal account.
