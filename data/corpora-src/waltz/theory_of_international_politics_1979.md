# Waltz 1979 — Theory of International Politics

## Argument

The foundational statement of neorealism, and the theoretical basis for Waltz's nuclear positions
rather than a work about nuclear weapons.

Waltz argues that international outcomes are explained by the structure of the international
system rather than by the attributes of its units. Structure has three elements: the ordering
principle, which internationally is anarchy rather than hierarchy; the functional
differentiation of units, which under anarchy is minimal because all states must provide for
their own security; and the distribution of capabilities, which is the only element that varies
meaningfully. Explanations resting on regime type, ideology, leaders' personalities or domestic
politics are therefore reduction rather than theory.

The consequences he draws include that states balance rather than bandwagon, that bipolar systems
are more stable than multipolar ones because there are fewer relationships to misjudge and no
allies whose defection changes the balance, and that self-help behaviour is compelled by the
structure regardless of what states would prefer.

For nuclear questions the relevance is that it supplies the reason Waltz expects prudence from any
nuclear-armed state whatever its character: structural pressure, not learning or culture or
institutional quality, produces the caution. This is what makes his position on proliferation
follow from his theory rather than being an independent judgement, and it is also the premise
Sagan denies by insisting organisations behave in ways structure does not predict.

For a crisis scenario the direct content is thin — the theory is about system-level outcomes over
time, not about decisions in a thirteen-day confrontation. Its value in the panel is as the
foundation of the Waltz positions in the 1981 and 1990 documents, and as the clearest statement of
the anti-organisational premise.

## Claims as stated

- International outcomes are explained by system structure rather than unit attributes.
- Structure comprises ordering principle, functional differentiation and distribution of
  capabilities.
- Anarchy is the international ordering principle; units are therefore functionally alike.
- Only the distribution of capabilities varies meaningfully.
- Explanations from regime type, ideology or leaders' personalities are reduction, not theory.
- States balance against power rather than bandwagoning with it.
- Bipolar systems are more stable than multipolar ones.
- Self-help behaviour is compelled by structure regardless of state preference.
- Structural pressure, not learning or institutional quality, produces prudence.

## Verify

The three elements of structure and the balancing claim should be taken from the text. The nuclear
implications drawn in the third paragraph are inference from Waltz's later work rather than content
of this book, and must be marked as such. Low direct relevance to a crisis scenario.
